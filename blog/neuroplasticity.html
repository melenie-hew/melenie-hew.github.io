Neuroplasticity

Discuss how understanding the principles of neuroplasticity benefits people.

Encouraging the brain’s neuroplasticity helps us to learn new things as well as enhance existing cognitive capabilities. It strays away from the thinking that brains are hard-wired at birth and unchangeable. It also highlights that people aren’t just ‘smart’ or ‘dumb’, that neural pathways in the brain are ever-changing and you can become better at something simply through practice.

Discuss how you might engage with the principles of neuroplasticity for your benefit.

I think the principles of neuroplasticity would help me shift my mindset from thinking that these concepts which we are learning are ‘too hard’ and that ‘my brain is not built for them’ to thinking that I can and I will eventually understand these concepts in due time and with enough practice.

What are some of the ways to increase your neuroplasticity?

Through the A.G.E.S learning model

  • A - attention - this is about minimising distractions in order to maximize attention when we are learning about something.
  • G - generation - this encourages us to form meaningful connections between what we are learning and what we already know or a skill we already have.
  • E - emotion - attaching the learning to an emotion or rewarding yourself after learning a particular thing may motivate you to keep working at something.
  • S - spacing - breaking down complex concepts to bite-sized pieces of information may help to move the information from short-term memory to long-term memory.

Growth Mindset

Discuss what it is and why it is relevant.

A growth mindset refers to the understanding that you are on a learning curve and an insight into the future. That you can get to where you need to be, just ‘not yet’. Instead of thinking that if a challenge is too hard, that you have failed the challenge. This is relevant to learning new and challenging things. We are bound to run into challenges and stops along the way, but the key is to adopt the ‘not yet’ growth mindset that affirms us that we will make it through, that we just need to give it some more time and effort.
