Problem Solving

A time when I was blocked on a simple problem

I had to add a property to another property and make that new added property an object. I knew how to give an object a particular property, but struggled when it came to making that property an object. An analogy for this problem is like with a Russian doll. I had the biggest Russian doll provided for me and I was tasked to create smaller Russian dolls but I also had to assign these smaller Russian dolls characteristics of their own.

What problem-solving techniques did you use?

I first tried to re-read the question several times out loud to see if I had missed anything. I also did a lot of googling and was in the MDN webdocs pages quite a lot. But because I felt the problem at hand was so specific, I ended up reaching out for help in the help desk channel on discord.

How did you feel throughout the process?

At the start of figuring out the problem, I felt confident that I would eventually get to the answer as I felt like it was a simple enough problem. Then when I got stuck for close to 30 minutes, I was getting increasingly frustrated as I was no closer to figuring it out and in fact, was starting to over complicate things.

What did you learn?

I learnt that it's okay to ask for help despite doing ‘easier’ challenges because only by understanding these smaller tasks, will I be able to work on the bigger ones.

What is a time I elegantly solved a problem. What was the problem?

In the ‘variables and objects’ kata, I felt it built my confidence a little bit as I knew what the question was asking and by checking the index.test.js file, I was able to piece the solution together. One example was when we were told to assign a spouse key to the terah object and to assign its value to adam. As simple as this problem was, I had to know what an object was and the concept of key-value pairs.

What problem-solving techniques did you use?

I looked back at some of the javascript challenges from sprint 3 and tried to work things out myself. I reflected on the steps I had taken previously in sprint 3 for similar challenges reflected on what I knew from the previous sprint.

How did you feel throughout the process?

I felt like I was comfortable with what the question was asking and also felt proud of myself when I got to complete and I saw that I was passing the tests.

What did you learn?

I learnt that a growth mindset is indeed very important. Because I started off thinking that even if I couldn’t figure things out immediately, that I would be able to at some point, just ‘not yet’.

Reflect on how confident you feel using each of these problem-solving techniques/processes:

Pseudocode:

Not confident yet. I used minimal amounts of this as more often than not, I would know what the question was asking but would not know where to start to begin solving the problem. For example, I would wonder if what I needed was a for loop or a for each loop or not a loop at all.

Trying something:

Quite confident with this. Definitely did a lot of ‘trying something’. With the kata challenges, I was trying to read into the test feedback as to why my solution wasn’t passing the test and tried different ways to work on that feedback.

Rubber ducky method:

I was talking aloud to myself quite a bit throughout this sprint. From reading the question out loud, to reading the steps I had already taken to see if that would help me figure out the next steps. So, quite confident with this.

Reading error messages:

Not very confident with this. Although I did a lot of reading of the error messages, I found the error messages helpful for simpler problems. But I found that when it came back as ‘undefined’, that didn’t bring me any closer to figuring out the solution.

Console.logging:

I don’t console.log enough. I should definitely be making more use of this to help build confidence and to help me be more comfortable console.logging things.

Googling:

I am pretty confident in googling to try and find. I did a lot of googling throughout the sprint and I felt I phrased questions appropriately to get the website/resources I needed.

Asking your peers for help:

Not too confident as there’s a fear of not wanting to seem like I don’t know my stuff. But I should definitely get more used to doing this.

Asking coaches for help:

I am still trying to build more confidence in doing this. I tend to exhaust all my options first and if I’ve been stuck on the same challenge for more than 30 to 40 minutes, is only when I ask the facilitators for help.

Improving your process with reflection:

I am also still trying to be more confident with my work through the process of reflecting. I do find that reflecting can be quite challenging, not because I don’t want to, but because of time constraints.


Reflect on a time you were reluctant to ask for help. Consider what made you reluctant to do so. What might you try differently next time?

I was very reluctant to ask for help after having asked for help through pretty much every single kata challenge. I felt that I was asking a lot unnecessarily, and that maybe to others, it would seem like I hadn’t even tried to solve the problems myself before posting in the help desk. I would like to put aside all reservations and thoughts of what others might think about me and post in the help desk anyway.
